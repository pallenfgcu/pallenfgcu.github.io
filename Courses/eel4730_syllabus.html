Course Catalog Description:

EEL 4730, Embedded Programming, gives students an in-depth, hands-on understanding of the interplay between software and hardware in embedded systems. The course covers the practical aspects of designing, developing, and optimizing software for embedded environments, using the C/C++ programming language together with AVR-based Arduino microcontrollers.

Students work through the essential concepts of hardware/software integration, GPIO and analog I/O, communication protocols (UART, SPI, I2C), process scheduling and state machines, real-time constraints, interrupt-driven programming, and code optimization for resource-constrained systems. Lecture and hands-on lab work build toward a team Group Project that applies these concepts to a real-world embedded application.

Learning Outcomes:

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  • LO1 Integrate hardware and software components to build functioning embedded systems.
  • LO2 Implement process scheduling and task management within resource-constrained embedded environments.
  • LO3 Apply code optimization strategies appropriate to memory- and processing-constrained systems.
  • LO4 Program external I/O devices and communication protocols (GPIO, analog I/O, UART, SPI, I2C).
  • LO5 Apply embedded systems concepts to a real-world application through a team project.

Graduates of this course will be well-prepared to contribute to industries that rely on embedded systems, such as consumer electronics, automotive, healthcare, and industrial automation.

Instructional Methods:

This is a face-to-face course with lectures, instructor led programming examples, and in-class labs.

Attendance is mandatory for all classes. Attendance will be taken and will count towards your Assignments grade. Students should notify me at least 48 hours in advance of intent to miss a class to arrange alternate schedules for in-class assignments.

Communication:

All course related communication should be via Canvas message. Each student is responsible for checking his/her FGCU email at least once a day. The instructor will respond to your emails within 24 hours except weekends and holidays.

Topics / Tentative Schedule:

  • Foundations of Embedded Systems: hardware/software boundaries, embedded vs. general-purpose computing, and the design constraints (cost, power, size, real-time) that shape embedded systems.
  • GPIO, Analog I/O & Actuators: digital and analog input/output, driving actuators, and interfacing with sensors.
  • Communication Protocols: UART, SPI, and I2C for device-to-device and device-to-host communication.
  • Exam 1 (covers all topics to this point)
  • Scheduling & State Machines: task scheduling and state machine design for embedded control flow.
  • Real-Time Constraints: worst-case execution time (WCET), jitter, and watchdog timers for responsive, reliable systems.
  • Interrupts: interrupt-driven programming for rapid, resource-efficient response to hardware events.
  • Optimization & Resource-Constrained Programming: code and memory optimization strategies for constrained embedded platforms.
  • Exam 2 (covers all topics since Exam 1)
  • Group Project: team-based design and implementation of an embedded system applying the semester's concepts.
  • Group Project Demonstrations (final exam period): in-class demonstration of completed Group Projects. There is no cumulative final exam.

Assessment:

There is no grace period for late submission of assignments. All assignments with due dates and times will be posted on Canvas. Alternate schedules can be arranged ahead of time with instructor approval.

Working Independently:

Students are to work independently on all non-group assignments. It is cheating to see other classmate's work or let other classmates see yours. This does not mean you cannot assist or discuss assignments with your classmates.

*** IMPORTANT ***

Student submissions that contain work from another student or source will receive a zero for that assignment. A second occurrence will result in failing the class, and the academic sanction will be reported to the Dean of Students.

Generative AI Use Policy:

Use of Generative AI tools (such as ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, etc.) is permitted in this course, but only within strict boundaries that support learning without compromising academic integrity or the intent of the assignments.

Permitted Use:

  • Generative AI may be used to assist with understanding a specific concept, algorithm, or section of provided code.
  • Prompts must be narrowly focused (for example, explaining how an algorithm works or clarifying what a provided code segment does).
  • You may include code from the textbook, lecture materials, or other sources in a prompt only for the purpose of understanding it.
  • AI may be used to clarify or reinforce understanding, but not to generate code or complete any portion of an assignment.

Prohibited Use:

  • Do not prompt with entire assignments, assignment questions, or large portions of an assignment.
  • Do not use AI to generate code that is submitted as part of your work.
  • Do not use AI to debug, improve, or modify code that you are submitting.

Disclosure Requirement:

  • You must submit a copy of all AI prompts and corresponding responses along with your assignment, including coding assignments.
  • Submissions without accompanying AI usage documentation will be treated as not using AI.

Violations:

  • First violation: zero on the assignment.
  • Second violation or use of AI beyond what was disclosed may result in a formal academic integrity referral.

This policy is intended to support learning while ensuring that submitted work reflects your own understanding and effort.
